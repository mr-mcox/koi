## What we're changing

*(Reframed 2026-11-03; the original framing is superseded by F9 and F17.)*

Target choice becomes a model judgment, made **once per research opportunity**, that states what we're trying to learn as a research question bound to one rubric dimension (operator's example: "How mature is the AI capability at company X? Do they have underlying data to feed it? Do they have appropriate leadership buy-in?"). That question then drives `DecidePlan`, and the mechanics keep it on a tight leash, widened when it's producing assertions and capped either way. The question step sees the rubric, past queries alongside the assertions they produced, and how likely each dimension is to yield. The outbound-link backlog moves to its own feature.

## Findings

- **F1** — `src/screen/baml_src/research.baml:31-100` — `DecidePlan` already receives `rubric_text`, `primary_target`, `last_context_text`, and `prior_queries`. So the planner already knows what it is falsifying/validating against, plus a little history of what it has tried.
- **F2** — `src/screen/research/state.py:18-56` and `src/screen/research/batch.py:110-126` — pass state already carries the right raw ingredients for a longer leash: `visited_urls`, `prior_queries`, `active_target`, `active_target_actions`, `active_target_action_cap`, and `target_stall_counts`. The batch engine sets the cap from `scoring_config.research_block_turns`, so a target can keep the pass for several actions before re-ranking.
- **F3** — `src/screen/research/baml_planner.py:81-113` — target choice is currently deterministic. The planner ranks targets from assertion-derived uncertainty plus stall suppression, then chooses the top one (or keeps the sticky target until the cap is hit). There is no candidate shortlist or explicit model choice among multiple targets yet.
- **F4** — `src/screen/research/dispatcher.py:51-89` and `src/screen/research_trace/events.py:31-59` — `DecidePlanRequest` records `targets_covered` and `target_assertion_counts`, which means traces can already tell us what had been discovered at each decision point and whether a target grew during a run. That is enough to measure yield-by-target from existing traces without inventing new history fields.
- **F5** — `src/screen/research/context.py:15-40` and `src/screen/research/baml_planner.py:34-57` — the last action context is already visible to the model, including search hits (with titles/URLs/snippets) and fetch snippets plus `targets_added`. This gives the planner some link-following visibility today, but it is still stringified context, not a structured backlog of candidate outbound URLs.
- **F6** — `src/screen/research/dispatcher.py:191-259` — the only supported actions are `search`, `fetch`, and `stop`. “Navigate to a link from a prior page” is already just `fetch`, but the planner only gets that option if the link appears in the last context text or it re-finds it via search; there is no separate navigation action and no persistent candidate-URL queue.
- **F7** — `src/screen/research_trace/replay.py:116-149` — the trace replay already reconstructs per-target stalls from `decide_plan` events. That makes it possible to derive target-specific yield/stall history from existing traces, which is the missing ingredient if we want the planner to prefer dimensions that actually produce assertions.
- **F8** — `src/screen/baml_src/research.baml:42-100` (path corrected; the smoke tests are `:103-256`) — the current prompt already has a long leash for page mining within a target: a fetch can keep revealing relevant links, and the planner is told not to stop just because the last action did not immediately produce a new assertion. So the missing piece is not the leash itself; it is better surfacing of promising follow-on URLs and better target-opportunity ranking.

### Second round (2026-11-03, after the first implementation attempt was reset)

- **F9** — operator — target choice should be a **model judgment** made in a separate step that writes down what it is trying to learn, in research-question form (e.g. "How mature is AI capability at Company X? Is there underlying data to feed it? Is there leadership buy-in?"). That question then drives `DecidePlan`, and the mechanics keep it on a tight leash. Rejected: handing `DecidePlan` a `primary_target` plus a candidate list, because a forced primary makes the candidates noise. Also rejected: a deterministic shortlist carrying `is_stalled` / `assertions_found` fields, because stalls are already folded into the ranking and counts are cheap evidence the model doesn't need as fields.
- **F10** — operator — "nothing to see here" is ended **mechanically**, by turns without an assertion (the existing stall mechanism), not by the model declaring it. The operator said this is probably better than a model-side exit.
- **F11** ⚠ [x] — operator agreed to loosen the girder (F19) → §Agreed. — `docs/architecture/README.md:28-30` (girder) and `src/screen/baml_src/research.baml:1` — "`DecidePlan` is the only LLM-shaped decision." A separate target/question-formulation call is a second LLM-shaped decision in the research loop. The girder's stated purpose is that the dispatcher owns every tool call and all state mutation; a call that only *returns* a question, with the dispatcher still owning when it runs and what it mutates, keeps that purpose but breaks the girder's literal wording. That is an escalation and needs a decisions.md entry.
- **F12** — `scoring.yaml:62-70`, `src/screen/research/batch.py:124,329`, `src/screen/intake/pipeline.py:198` — `research_block_turns` (5) sets **both** the per-pass turn budget and `active_target_action_cap`; the yaml says they "are the same quantity … so they move together." Each resume builds a fresh `LoopState` with `active_target=None` (`batch.py:110-126`). In practice, then, **one pass = one target**: `select_primary_target` ranks once at turn 0 and the sticky leash holds for the rest of the block. A question-formulation step at target-selection time therefore fires about **once per pass** (one extra LLM call per ≤5 turns), not once per turn.
- **F13** — `src/screen/research_trace/replay.py:33-35,50-66,116-125` — stall accounting is keyed by target slug: runs are segmented on `active_target or primary_target`, and a stall is "this slug's assertion count did not rise across the run." Assertions also carry exactly one `target` slug (`src/screen/types.py:86`, `Target` Literal). So whatever the new step emits must still resolve to **one slug** per run, or stall detection (F10's exit) has nothing to key on.
- **F14** [x] — operator: **one dimension per question**; assertions landing on other dimensions are an accepted side effect, not a goal. F13's per-slug stall accounting stays as is → §Agreed. Original question: does a research question bind to **one** target slug, or may it span several? F9's example (AI maturity / data / leadership buy-in) plausibly touches more than one dimension (e.g. `agentic`, `craft_direction`). If it spans several, F13's per-slug stall accounting needs a new key (the question itself?) and that is a trace-shape change under E8.
- **F15** [x] — operator: the step sees (a) the rubric, the thing evidence is gathered against; (b) past queries, ideally *correlated with the assertion text they produced*; (c) a per-dimension aggregate of how likely research is to produce assertions (operator's example: `internal_culture` fails a lot); (d) what would move that opportunity → §Approach. Candidates: the rubric (already given to `DecidePlan`, F1); prior queries (F2); existing assertions per target, as chunks or counts; and stall history (F7). F9 asks for "fuzzy signals — previous queries and what assertions resulted." **The planner sees none of the assertions today** (`baml_planner.py:129-142` passes only rubric, target, last context and queries), so this is new input whichever way it's answered.
- **F16** [x] — operator: **retire** the deterministic ranker; it isn't saving much. The model step replaces it outright, not as pre-filter or fallback → §Agreed. Original question: is the deterministic ranker (`rank_targets`, `baml_planner.py:81-96`) kept as a pre-filter (the model chooses among its top-N), kept as the fallback when the call fails, or retired? This decides whether S-curve suppression (`score/bandit.py:41-49`) still shapes target choice or only shapes the stall exit.

Original question: is the outbound-link backlog (original Done When #2, F5/F6) still in this feature, or does it split into its own node? It doesn't depend on who picks the target.

### Third round (2026-11-03, after the operator answered F14-F17)

- **F19** — operator — loosening the F11 girder is agreed. In the operator's words it is "similar shape, but extending further": the dispatcher still owns every tool call and every state mutation, and LLM-shaped decisions become two, `DecidePlan` plus the new question step. Needs a decisions.md entry and an update to the README girder line → §Agreed.
- **F20** — operator — **target selection happens once per research opportunity**: when a batch draw picks an opening, it also picks one research question. When the block ends, the next draw picks a new opening *and* a new question. This matches what the code already does (F12), so it's a formalization, not a behavior change → §Agreed.
- **F21** [!] — operator — wants a **widened leash**: if the question is producing assertions, it may run past the base block, up to a hard cap; if it's coming up dry, it ends at the base (or earlier, per F10). Concern on both sides: don't cut off good progress, don't burn turns on dry searches → §Approach / §Recalibrate When. Both numbers are unmeasured, so per AGENTS.md "measure before model" they are named config values in `scoring.yaml`, not constants.
- **F22** — `src/screen/research/batch.py:329`, `:308`, `:441-470` — the batch block is `min(research_block_turns, batch_size - total_spent)`, decided *before* the pass runs. A leash that extends on yield has to be decided *inside* the pass (the dispatcher's budget check, `dispatcher.py:152-159`). The batch's remaining budget still caps it, so an extension can never overspend the batch. Today `turn_budget` and `active_target_action_cap` are both `research_block_turns` (F12); collapsing them to one per-question budget with an extension rule removes `active_target_action_cap` as a separate concept.
- **F23** — `src/screen/research_trace/replay.py:69-70,116-125`, `src/screen/score/bandit.py:39-47` — **what exists for "likelihood to produce assertions" is a stall count, not a rate.** `target_stalls[slug]` counts contiguous runs on a slug that added none, *per opening trace only*; there is no count of successful runs, no denominator, and nothing aggregated across openings. The operator's "internal culture fails a lot" therefore isn't currently computable as a rate. Per opening, runs and stalls are both derivable from existing `decide_plan` events (`_segment_target_runs` already yields every run), so runs-attempted / runs-that-yielded per slug needs no new trace field. **Cross-opening** aggregation would mean reading every trace under `data/research_traces/` (`trace_io.py:19`), one file per opening.
- **F24** [x] — settled by F32: pool-wide yield over all 201 live traces computes in seconds from existing `decide_plan` events, so cost no longer separates the options; proposed as pool-wide plus this opening's own runs → §Approach. Original question: is the yield signal (F15c) **per opening** (this opening's own history on each dimension) or **pool-wide** (how `internal_culture` research goes across all openings)? The operator's example reads as pool-wide: "internal culture has a pretty high failure rate" is a statement about the dimension, not about one company. Pool-wide is new I/O (F23) and a stronger prior early in an opening's life; per-opening is free but empty on a fresh opening.
- **F25** — `src/screen/research_trace/events.py:31-59` (DecidePlanRequest), `src/screen/research/context.py:28-40` (FetchContext) — **query→assertion correlation (F15b) is reconstructable without a new field, but only loosely.** Within one trace, events are ordered: `tavily_search(q)` → `decide_plan` → `tavily_extract(url)` → the next `decide_plan`, whose `target_assertion_counts` delta shows what that fetch added. Assertion text lives in SQLite, not the trace. Each assertion's `citations[].url` (`types.py:111-131`, extract.baml:12,45) is the fetched page URL, so assertion → fetch URL → nearest preceding search query is a join across trace and DB. The weak link: a fetched URL isn't recorded as coming *from* a particular search hit (the planner may have pulled it out of a fetch snippet), so the attribution is "most recent search before this fetch," which is a heuristic.
- **F26** [x] — F34 shows 91% of planner fetches are URLs that appeared in a prior search's hits, so the join is mostly exact membership, not a heuristic; proposed as (b) → §Approach. Original question: how is the query→assertion pairing rendered to the model? Options: (a) per dimension, the assertions' `chunk` text plus their source URLs, with past queries listed separately, so the model correlates fuzzily (no join); (b) the F25 join, rendered as "query → URLs fetched → assertions produced". (a) is cheap and needs no attribution heuristic; (b) is what the operator literally described, but its correctness depends on the heuristic.
- **F27** — `src/screen/digest/service.py:43-75`, `src/screen/baml_src/digest.baml` — a cached ≤30-word prose digest per `(opening, dimension)` already exists and regenerates only when that dimension's assertion count grows. That's a compact, already-paid-for rendering of "what we know per dimension" the question step could read instead of raw chunks. It isn't model-free (the digest is LLM output), but it's cached and already consumed by the compare UI.
- **F28** — operator (F15d) — "what would move that opportunity" is open to two readings: (i) **what evidence would change the dimension's standing**, i.e. VOI in decisions W3's sense ("would this change what happens"), which the scorer can hint at via `TargetStats.half_width` (`score/scorer.py:34-75`) and the opening's top-K boundary (`batch.py:206-226`); or (ii) **what would change the yield odds**, e.g. "internal culture searches fail on review sites; try engineering blogs." ### Fourth round (2026-11-03, measured on `data/live/research_traces/`, read-only, operator-permitted for this session)

201 traces, 632 fetched pages with content. Runs are contiguous `decide_plan` events sharing `active_target or primary_target`. A step is productive when the target's count in `target_assertion_counts` rises between consecutive `decide_plan` events. Runs whose end has no following snapshot are excluded. Scripts were ad hoc and are not committed. `n` is small for several dimensions, so the bands are wide.

- **F32** — live traces — **per-dimension run yield (runs that added ≥1 assertion for their own target / runs):** location 3/12 (25%), internal_culture 10/24 (42%), domain 21/43 (49%), mission 8/16 (50%), compensation 27/48 (56%), agentic 31/55 (56%), craft_direction 4/5, schematic 1/2, trajectory 1/1. Retired slugs also appear (`non_scoring:obtainability` 14/17, `extractive_business` 1/2), so any aggregate must drop slugs outside the current `Target` Literal (decisions R9 does the same at the DB boundary). The operator's "internal culture fails a lot" holds, and location fails more. Thin dimensions (≤5 runs) need a prior, not a raw rate (AGENTS.md: no unearned precision).
- **F33** ⚠-adjacent [!] — live traces — **yield arrives early and doesn't compound.** P(productive) by step within a run, for runs with no yield yet: step 1 4% (almost always a search), **step 2 33%** (almost always the first fetch), steps 3-5 10-14%, step 6+ 6%. For runs that **already yielded**: step 3 3%, step 4 10%, step 5 13%, step 6+ 9%. A productive run is *not* more likely to keep producing than a dry one, so F21's "extend while it's finding assertions" has no support in this data. Of 82 runs that reached 5 steps, the 5th step produced in 7. Caveat: the current prompt tells the planner to stop once a fetch adds assertions for the target (`research.baml:48-51`), so post-yield behavior is shaped by that rule and may change when the step is driven by a question instead of a slug. → §Recalibrate When.
- **F34** — live traces — **fetches are where yield comes from; searches aren't.** First-fetch steps yield about 50% (107/204 at step 2; fetches at steps 3-5 yield 35-50%), and searches yield almost nothing (step-1 yield 4%, mostly from the 21 fetch-first runs). So "turns without an assertion" (F10) mixes two different things: a search that sets up a fetch is not a dry turn in the way a fetch that adds nothing is. Of 532 planner fetches, 482 (91%) were URLs from a prior search's hits, 9 (1.7%) were URLs seen in a previously fetched page, and 41 came from elsewhere.
- **F35** — live traces — dry streaks: of yields that came after a dry streak, the streak was 1 step in 117 cases, 2 in 21, 3 in 17, 4 in 8, and ≥7 in 7. A "stop after k consecutive dry steps" rule at k=3 would have cut off 8+7=15 late yields across 201 traces. Measured in steps, not fetches.

## Notes / implications

- A richer target object full of prose is probably not the right first move; the current system already has enough text surfaces.
- The more useful shape is likely: a small ranked set of candidate targets plus compact per-target stats, and a separate structured backlog of outbound links harvested from pages.
- Existing traces should remain readable by keeping any new fields optional and deriving the new rankings from trace-replay-compatible history rather than rewriting the trace format.
- (2026-11-03) The first bearing's Approach #2 ("deterministic … do not add another model-generated pre-summary layer") and Not Doing #1 are directly contradicted by F9; the bearing needs a rewrite, not a patch.
